Mentor Expectations

Clear guidelines for our PromathsBridge mentors

Your Role as a Mentor

As a PromathsBridge mentor, your primary responsibility is to guide first-year students through their transition to university life. You are not expected to be an academic tutor, but rather a supportive peer who can share experiences and provide guidance.

Regular Check-ins & Reports

Submit brief monthly reports about your mentorship sessions and check-ins with your mentee.

Transition Support Focus

Help first-years adapt to university life - campus navigation, time management, resource awareness, and social integration.

Academic Guidance (Optional)

While not required, you may offer study tips and course advice if you feel comfortable. Academic tutoring is NOT a prerequisite.

Guidance & Encouragement

Provide emotional support, share personal experiences, and encourage mentees to make the most of their university journey.

Key Points to Remember

What makes a great mentor

You're a guide, not a tutor

Focus on transition support rather than academic instruction

Regular communication

Monthly check-ins and reports help track progress

Share experiences, not answers

Your journey through university is your most valuable asset

Build confidence

Help mentees develop independence and university life skills

Tutor Expectations

Clear guidelines for our PromathsBridge tutors

Your Role as a Tutor

As a PromathsBridge tutor, your primary responsibility is to provide academic support and help first-year students with their school work. You are expected to assist with understanding course material, completing assignments, and preparing for exams.

Regular Reports & Session Logs

Submit detailed monthly reports documenting tutoring sessions, topics covered, and student progress.

Academic Support Focus

Provide assistance with school work including homework help, concept explanations, study strategies, and exam preparation.

Subject Expertise

Demonstrate proficiency in the subjects/modules you tutor. You may be asked to provide evidence of your academic performance in these areas.

Structured Sessions

Plan and conduct organized tutoring sessions with clear learning objectives and follow-up activities.

Key Points to Remember

What makes an effective tutor

Academic support is primary

Focus on helping with school work, assignments, and exam prep

Documentation is required

Regular reports help track academic progress and session effectiveness

Clear communication

Explain concepts in ways that are accessible to first-year students

Patience and encouragement

Build confidence in students' academic abilities

Subject mastery

Ensure you have strong understanding of the subjects you tutor
